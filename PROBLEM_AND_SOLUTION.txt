CAN I AFFORD THIS ORDER?
A liquidity check for companies that buy before they get paid

Built with Zwapgrid + Open Payments


------------------------------------------------------------
THE PROBLEM
------------------------------------------------------------

Middleman companies buy goods from suppliers and resell them to
customers. They pay their suppliers before their customers pay them.

That gap is where things go wrong.

A company places an order with a supplier. Thirty days later the
supplier invoice is due. But the money from their own customers
hasn't arrived yet, because those customers pay late. Now the
company can't pay, and it becomes someone else's problem: late fees,
a damaged supplier relationship, or a supplier who stops delivering.

The frustrating part is that nobody set out to overcommit. The
decision to order was made with incomplete information.

Here is why. When someone asks "can we afford this?", they look at
the bank balance. The bank balance looks fine. So they order.

But the bank account has no idea that:
  - 400,000 SEK of supplier invoices come due over the next 3 weeks
  - payroll runs on the 25th
  - a VAT payment is due next month
  - the big customer invoice they're counting on is always paid late

And the accounting system, which does know all of that, has no idea
how much money is actually in the bank right now.

So the two systems that could answer the question never talk to each
other. The company is left guessing.


------------------------------------------------------------
THE INSIGHT
------------------------------------------------------------

The bank knows what you HAVE.
The accounting system knows what you OWE and what you're OWED.

Neither one can answer "can I afford this order?" on its own.
Together, they can answer it completely.


------------------------------------------------------------
THE SOLUTION
------------------------------------------------------------

A tool where a company types in an order they're thinking about
placing, and gets a straight answer:

  YES  - place it now, you're covered
  NO   - but you can place it on September 14th
  RISKY - it works only if your biggest customer pays on time

How it works, in plain terms:

1. We read the real bank balance and transaction history through
   Open Payments. This is the actual money, across every account
   and bank the company uses, including payments that are already
   on their way out but haven't settled yet.

2. We read the company's invoices through Zwapgrid. Every unpaid
   supplier invoice they owe, with its due date. Every unpaid
   customer invoice they're waiting on, with its due date.

3. We learn how customers actually behave. Zwapgrid gives us the
   payment history, so we can see that a particular customer pays
   twelve days late, every single time. We stop pretending that
   invoice will arrive on its due date, because it won't.

4. We build a day-by-day forecast of the bank balance for the next
   three months. Money in, money out, on the dates it will really
   happen rather than the dates it was promised.

5. We drop the proposed order into that forecast on the day its
   invoice would come due, and check whether the balance ever dips
   below a safety cushion.

If it does, we don't just say no. We say when. We roll the order
forward day by day until it fits, and tell the company the earliest
date they can safely place it.

We also tell them WHY. Not "insufficient funds", but "you're 42,000
SEK short on September 3rd, because of the VAT payment and the
invoice from Nordic Metals." That's something a person can act on.


------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT MAKES IT MORE THAN A DASHBOARD
------------------------------------------------------------

Open Payments can also move money, including scheduling payments
for a future date. So when we say "you can place this order if you
pay on September 14th", we can schedule that payment right there.

The answer becomes an action instead of a warning.

And because we can see the real bank transactions, we can match
them against the invoices in the accounting system and mark them
as paid automatically. The forecast stays accurate without waiting
for someone to do the bookkeeping.


------------------------------------------------------------
WHY IT MATTERS
------------------------------------------------------------

Every company in this position is already making this decision.
They're just making it with a bank balance and a gut feeling.

We're not asking them to change how they operate. We're giving them
the one number they were missing at the moment they needed it.
